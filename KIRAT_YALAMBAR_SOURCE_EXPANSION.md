# Kirat Worldwide / Yalambar Evidence Expansion

This additive research index records sources identified in the current web research pass. It does NOT assert that this is an exhaustive worldwide bibliography. Exact quotations and page/folio evidence must be checked against the original source before publication.

## Yalambar / Yalamba / Yellung

1. **Sylvain Lévi — The History of Nepal / Histoire du Népal (Le Népal)**
   - Source discusses Yellung (K.), Yalamba, Yalambar as the first of the Kiratas in Nepalese chronicle tradition.
   - Search evidence: Ancient Nepal / Kailash OCR reproduces the discussion and notes the variant forms.
   - Evidence target: original volume/page scan; preserve the original spelling.
   - Status: primary text identified; exact edition/page to verify before publication.

2. **William Kirkpatrick — An Account of the Kingdom of Nepaul (1811)**
   - His chronicle/genealogical material is cited in later historical works for the Kirata dynasty and Yellung/Yalambar chronology.
   - Nepal National Archives hosts a digitized PDF.
   - Evidence target: original 1811 pages, especially the Kirata genealogy/chronicle section.
   - Status: original digitized source identified; exact page must be checked from the PDF.

3. **Ancient Nepal / Department of Archaeology historical scholarship**
   - Discusses Yellung/Yalamba/Yalambar and the Kirata dynasty as represented in Nepalese vamsavali traditions, while distinguishing chronicle tradition from independently contemporary evidence.
   - Evidence target: original journal page(s).

4. **Hasrat — Introduction to the History of Nepal / History of Nepal as told by Its Own and Contemporary Chroniclers**
   - Discusses Yalamba as the Kirata ruler of eastern Nepal who, in chronicle tradition, took control of Nepal Proper.
   - This is a later historical compilation and must be attributed as such.
   - Evidence target: original PDF pages.

5. **Gopala/Vamsavali traditions**
   - Various Nepalese genealogical chronicles contain different Kirata king lists and chronologies. Yalambar/Yellung appears as the first Kirata ruler in some traditions.
   - Evidence target: exact manuscript/edition/folio for each version; do not merge different genealogies.

## Broader Kirat evidence identified in this pass

6. **Francis Buchanan Hamilton (1819)** — Kirat/Kichak east of Nepal Proper; political status; customs/religion/language; Agam Singha and local informants. Original Gutenberg text is available.

7. **Brian Houghton Hodgson (1857/1859 and manuscripts)** — Comparative Kiranti vocabulary; On the Kiranti Tribe; original manuscript/archival materials on Kiranti languages, Limbu script, religion and customs.

8. **Martin Gaenszle (2021 and related works)** — Kiranti oral tradition, Mundhum, Limbu script and the distinction between oral ritual traditions and later scripturalisation.

9. **Liliya A. Streltsova (2023)** — Kirat religion; Limbu, Rai, Yakha and Sunuwar in wider Kiranti context; Mundhum and religious identity.

10. **Documenta Nepalica / Heidelberg Academy** — Pallo Kirata and related historical documents, including multiple dated archival records.

11. **TU / university theses** — Mundum/Mundhum, Kirat religion, Rai ritual and related Kiranti studies; each thesis must become an independent source record when its original PDF and relevant pages are verified.

## Publication rule

- Do not label any source as “proven truth” merely because a chronicle, later historian or oral tradition says it.
- Keep **source statement**, **evidence**, **scholarly interpretation**, **oral tradition**, and **researcher conclusion** separate.
- Preserve exact author/source spelling: Yellung, Yalamba, Yalambar, Yalang, Yalunga etc. should not be silently normalized.
- For each public record, the actual page/folio scan/PDF should be attached by the archive owner where rights permit.
