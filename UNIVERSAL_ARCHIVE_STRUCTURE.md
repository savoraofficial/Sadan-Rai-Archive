# Universal Archive Structure

The archive now defines **50 Universal Core Sections** for every civilization, religion, people, culture, kingdom, local history, oral-history subject and comparative research project.

Kirat's current 20 public/research sections remain preserved. They are a subject-specific presentation layer mapped into the universal architecture; they do not limit other subjects to 20 sections.

The 50 core sections are extensible. New specialized sections may be added without deleting or renaming the universal core.

## Public presentation rule — evidence-led, visitor-first
For any major civilization/religion/culture/history landing section, public presentation should surface a concise, readable synthesis first: what it is, how it formed, what evidence exists, what the strongest sources actually establish, independent cross-checks, and the researcher-controlled conclusion. The full source register, status matrix, restricted-object notes, and internal research audit remain in the research/evidence layer. Public pages must not turn internal status labels into visitor-facing clutter.
