# Sadan Rai Archive — Kirat Worldwide / Kirat Hang / Yalambar Research Register

**Collection date:** 2026-10-03
**Scope:** Kirat/Kirata/Kiranti history, Kirat Hang/Yalambar traditions, eastern Nepal, Kathmandu Valley traditions, Rai, Limbu/Yakthung, Yakkha, Sunuwar, Mundhum, language, scripts, inscriptions, chronicles, archival documents, ethnography and modern scholarship.

## Important evidence rule
This is a research register, not a declaration that every listed claim is historical fact. Each item is kept with its source type and attribution. Traditional/legendary claims, later chronicle claims, scholarly interpretations and contemporary/archival evidence must remain separate.

## Search coverage completed in this pass
Multiple independent web searches were run across archival, university, scholarly, government, library and community repositories. This is **not certified as a literally exhaustive worldwide bibliography**; newly discovered material should be appended rather than silently treated as absent.

## High-priority source records

### 1. Gopalrajavamsavali / Nepalese chronicles
- **Type:** Chronicle / later manuscript tradition
- **Topic:** Kirat dynasty and king lists; Yalambar/Yalamba/Yellung variants
- **Key evidence lead:** Witzel discusses the Gopalrajavamsavali (GRV), its Kirata king list, manuscript folios 17b.5–18b.1, and the non-Indo-Aryan-looking names.
- **Important caution:** The surviving written compilation is medieval; it is not a contemporary record of the alleged early Kirat reign.
- **Evidence URL:** https://fid4sa-repository.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/107/1/Nepaleshydro_1993.pdf

### 2. Sylvain Lévi — The History of Nepal / Le Népal
- **Type:** Secondary historical scholarship
- **Topic:** Kirata genealogies, Yellung/Yalamba/Yalambar, Pabi/Pamvi, chronology and relationship to chronicle traditions.
- **Key evidence lead:** Lévi discusses Yellung (K.), Yalamba, Yalambar and the chronicle tradition linking the first Kirata to the Yo-loung/Yar-loung legend.
- **Exact page lead:** Part I, p. 24 in the accessible OCR edition; verify against the original edition before treating pagination as final.
- **Evidence URL:** https://www.wisdomlib.org/history/compilation/kailash-journal-of-himalayan-studies/ocr/1602043/24

### 3. Michael Witzel — Linguistic Archaeology of the Nepal Valley
- **Type:** Scholarly article / linguistic-historical study
- **Topic:** GRV Kirata king names, Licchavi inscriptions, Tibeto-Burman language hypothesis, chronology and source criticism.
- **Key evidence lead:** GRV folios 17b.5–18b.1 are discussed, with a list of Kirata king names.
- **Evidence URL:** https://fid4sa-repository.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/107/1/Nepaleshydro_1993.pdf

### 4. Recently Discovered Inscriptions of Licchavi Nepal
- **Type:** Epigraphic study / primary inscription evidence
- **Topic:** occurrence of “kirātaveṣadhara” in a Licchavi inscription.
- **Key evidence lead:** The authors describe the entry as a contemporary reference to Kirāta, while explicitly cautioning that the word may alternatively be a generic term meaning “barbarian.”
- **Evidence URL:** https://sources.mandala.library.virginia.edu/sites/mandala-sources.lib.virginia.edu/files/pdf-files/693_0.pdf

### 5. Francis Buchanan Hamilton — An Account of the Kingdom of Nepal (1819)
- **Type:** Early European/British account; near-contemporary ethnographic/administrative observation
- **Topic:** Kirat/Kichak territory, political status, customs, language, Agam Singha, Lamas/Brahmans, independence and eastern Nepal.
- **Evidence lead:** Project Gutenberg edition; original 1819 pagination must be checked against a scan for final page citations.
- **Evidence URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/30364/pg30364-images.html

### 6. William Kirkpatrick — An Account of the Kingdom of Nepaul (1811)
- **Type:** Early British account
- **Topic:** Nepal, eastern peoples and traditional chronology/source material later used by Kirat historians.
- **Evidence lead:** Locate exact original pages for every Kirata/Yalambar statement before quotation.

### 29. Mahabharata / classical Sanskrit references to Kirāta
- **Type:** Ancient literary evidence
- **Topic:** uses of the term Kirāta in epic narratives and descriptions of Himalayan/frontier peoples.
- **Caution:** Ancient literary “Kirāta” must not automatically be equated with the modern Rai/Limbu/Kiranti population. That identification requires separate scholarly argument.
- **Evidence lead:** Lévi and later studies; use critical editions/authoritative translations for exact verse-level evidence.

### 30. Ramayana / classical references
- **Type:** Ancient literary evidence
- **Topic:** Kirāta in geographical/ethnographic lists.
- **Caution:** same source-identification rule as above; preserve the ancient term and context rather than retroactively assigning modern ethnicity.

### 31. Gopālarājavamsāvalī king-list tradition
- **Type:** Chronicle evidence
- **Topic:** traditional sequence of Kirata rulers, including lists that differ in count and chronology.
- **Caution:** different manuscript/chronicle traditions may report different totals; never merge them into one “confirmed” list without documenting the manuscript.

### 32. Yalambar / Yalamba / Yellung spelling family
- **Type:** Research normalization index only
- **Variants to preserve:** Yalambar, Yalamba, Yalanga/Yalang, Yellung, Yalunga and other forms encountered in individual sources.
- **Rule:** original spelling stays in the source record; standardized search aliases belong only in a separate index.

### 33. Kirat Hang / Hang terminology
- **Type:** Historical-cultural terminology
- **Topic:** later Kiranti/Limbu political and cultural use of Hang/Hangma and named rulers/chiefs.
- **Rule:** do not automatically back-project later Hang terminology into every ancient Kirata source; document the period and source in each record.

## Public archive rule

For modern copyrighted works, store metadata, short quotations where legally appropriate, researcher notes, and stable links rather than copying whole books/articles.
