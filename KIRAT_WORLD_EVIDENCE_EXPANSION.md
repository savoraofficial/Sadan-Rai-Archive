# Kirat Civilization — Worldwide Evidence Expansion

This is an additive research-source register. It does NOT assert that this is an exhaustive list of every work ever written. Each item must be verified against its original edition/document before publication.

## Newly verified evidence families

1. **Licchavi Inscription V / Kiratavesadhara** — contemporary epigraphic evidence discussed by Slusser. The reading “kiratavesadhara” is treated cautiously by Slusser because *kirata* can also be a generic term. Evidence target: original inscription image and scholarly transcription.
2. **Gopalarajavamsavali** — medieval chronicle tradition; the chronicle lists 32 Kirata kings and says the Kiratas later lived toward the Arun and Tamakoshi. Evidence target: facsimile, transcription and translation; do not present the king-list as independently proven chronology.
3. **Dhanavajra Vajracharya & Kamal P. Malla (eds./trans.)**, *The Gopalarajavamsavali* (1985). Facsimile, transcription, Nepali and English translations; National Archives/Nepal Research Centre edition.
4. **Sylvain Lévi**, *Le Népal, étude historique d'un royaume hindou* (1905–08). Evidence target: exact volume/page for every Kirata claim.
5. **Suniti Kumar Chatterji** — historical/linguistic identification work cited in scholarship concerning ancient Kirata and modern eastern Nepal peoples. Evidence target: exact original work/page.
6. **Robert Shafer** — linguistic/historical work cited in scholarship concerning Kirata and Tibeto-Burman evidence. Evidence target: exact original work/page.
7. **Michael Witzel** — research on early South Asian linguistics and the Nepal Valley; discusses the Gopalarajavamsavali, Kirata king-list and linguistic evidence. Evidence target: exact publication/page.
8. **Kamāl P. Malla** — research on Nepal Valley linguistic/epigraphic history and non-Sanskritic names relevant to the so-called Kirata period. Evidence target: exact publication/page.
9. **Mahesh Chandra Regmi**, “Some Questions on Nepali History: The Kiratas” — explicitly evaluates the evidentiary status of the Kirata king-list and distinguishes chronicle tradition from dependable epigraphic/documentary evidence. Evidence target: original article pages.
10. **Dhanavajra Vajracharya** — scholarship/edition work on Nepalese chronicles and early history; evidence target: exact work/page.
11. **Documenta Nepalica / Heidelberg Academy of Sciences** — Pallo Kirata records dated 1780, 1819, 1834, 1851 and related records. Evidence target: original digital folio for each selected document.
12. **Documenta Nepalica: appeal to Kiratis and Lepchas across the Tista (VS 1865)** — two-folio document, catalogued as RRC_0036_0252. Evidence target: both original folios.
13. **Regmi Research Collection / Documenta Nepalica** — land survey and revenue documents concerning Majha-Kirata and Pallo-Kirata. Evidence target: original document images and catalogue identifiers.
14. **Brian Houghton Hodgson**, *On the Kiranti Tribe* (1859), pp. 447–456.
15. **Brian Houghton Hodgson**, *Comparative Vocabulary of the several languages (dialects) of the celebrated people called Kirantis* (1857), Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal 26(5), pp. 333–371.
16. **Royal Asiatic Society Archives — Hodgson, Comparative Vocabulary of the Kiranti**, BHH/6/1/1: six large handwritten sheets, eleven sides, comparing English with multiple Kiranti languages/dialects including Yakkha and several Rai/Kiranti varieties.
17. **George van Driem**, research on Yakkha and Kiranti languages; his Yakkha study explicitly compares Yakkha verbal systems with other Kiranti languages and discusses Limbu, Yakkha and Rai groupings.
18. **Liliya A. Streltsova**, “The ‘Kirat Religion’ in Nepal: Quest for a New Religious and Ethnic Identity” (2023), pp. 66–83; treats Limbu, Rai, Yakha and Sunuwar in the wider Kiranti context and discusses Mundhum and religious change.
19. **Martin Gaenszle** — research on Mewahang Rai oral ritual texts, Mundhum, scripturalisation and Kiranti religious/oral traditions. Evidence target: exact book/article/chapter and page.
20. **Lionel Caplan**, *Land and Social Change in East Nepal* (1970) — Limbu, land/kipat and Hindu–tribal relations. Evidence target: exact pages for every claim.
21. **Tanka Bahadur Subba**, *Politics of Culture: A Study of Three Kirata Communities in the Eastern Himalayas* (1999) — Limbu, Rai and Yakkha; historical and ethnographic data.
22. **Philippe Sagant**, research on Limbu political power, religion and society. Evidence target: exact publication/page.
23. **Charlotte Hardman** — ethnographic research on Lohorung Rai and Yakkha/Kiranti communities. Evidence target: exact dissertation/publication/page.
24. **Gerd Hansson**, *The Rai of Eastern Nepal: Ethnic and Linguistic Grouping* (1991).
25. **Mélanie Vandenhelsken & Martin Gaenszle** — Limbu religion/community research. Evidence target: exact publication/page.
26. **Gérard Schlemmer**, “New Past for the Sake of a Better Future: Re-Inventing the History of the Kirant in East Nepal” (2003), European Bulletin of Himalayan Research 25/26, pp. 119–144.
27. **Gyanu Maya Rai & Yang Zhuhui**, “The Contribution of the Ancient Kirat Civilization in Nepal and its Consequences of Decline” — modern scholarly discussion of Kirat civilization, Mundum and cultural history. Evidence target: original article/pages; treat interpretations as authors’ interpretations.
28. **Mohan Kumar Tumbahang**, doctoral research on Limbu Mundhum — mythology, rituals, language, script and philosophy. Evidence target: thesis pages and cited primary texts.
29. **Iman Singh Chemjong**, *History and Culture of the Kirat People / Kirat History and Culture* — major Nepali/Kiranti compilation using European and indigenous sources. Evidence target: exact edition/page for each claim; distinguish Chemjong’s synthesis from earlier sources.
30. **Tanka Bahadur Rai**, *Kirat Itihas* / Kirat historical works. Evidence target: exact edition/page.
31. **Dor Bahadur Bista**, *People of Nepal* — Kiranti/Rai/Limbu ethnographic discussion. Evidence target: exact edition/page.
32. **National Archives / Nepali historical editions** — Gopalarajavamsavali and other documentary materials. Evidence target: original scan/facsimile and catalogue metadata.
33. **Hodgson Papers / Cambridge–South Asian sources** — Kiranti/Limbu manuscripts, script, prayers, maps, clan/family-name material and language teaching material. Evidence target: original manuscript folios/catalogue item.
34. **Limbu Mundhum printed/scriptural traditions** — printed and manuscript Mundhum texts, including Iman Singh Chemjong’s 1961 *Kirat Mundhum* and later Limbu-script publications. Evidence target: original edition/page or manuscript folio.
35. **Modern university theses/dissertations** — Rai, Limbu, Yakkha, Sunuwar/Kiranti history, culture, language, religion and oral tradition. Each thesis must be a separate source record with institutional repository, author, year and exact page.

## Evidence rule

For every claim: **Source → exact page/folio → original quotation/description → researcher translation → explanation → actual evidence file**. If the original page/folio has not been verified, the record must say `page verification required` rather than inventing a page.

## Important research distinction

Ancient *Kirata* references, medieval chronicle traditions, modern *Kiranti* ethnolinguistic identity, and present-day *Kirat religion* are related research domains but are not automatically identical historical claims. The archive must preserve the author/source’s wording and clearly label later interpretation.
