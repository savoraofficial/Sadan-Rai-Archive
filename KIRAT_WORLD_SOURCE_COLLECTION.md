# Sadan Rai Archive — Kirat Civilization World Source Collection

## Purpose

This is an additive, source-controlled master register for materials connected with Kirat/Kiranti civilization and its historical, social, political, religious, linguistic, oral, ethnographic, documentary, and material heritage.

**Archive rule:** A source is not treated as proof of a claim merely because it is listed here. Each source must be linked to its original page/folio/document and actual evidence before a specific claim is treated as documented.

**Evidence rule:** For every source/claim, upload the original page, scan, manuscript image, archival image, or stable documentary copy that supports the exact statement. Do not create evidence from a secondary summary.

**Language rule:** English fields use clean English. Nepali fields use clean Nepali. Original quotations remain in the original source language.

## Source families currently identified

### A. Early European / British / colonial-era accounts

1. Father Giuseppe (Capuchin missionary account) — early European account referenced by later writers for the status of the Kiratas in the eighteenth century. **Evidence target:** original missionary account or an authoritative archival reproduction; Hamilton's quotation is a secondary witness to Giuseppe and must not be treated as the original Giuseppe text.

2. Colonel William Kirkpatrick — *An Account of the Kingdom of Nepaul* (1811). **Evidence target:** original 1811 edition, exact page(s) mentioning Kirata/Kirantis or eastern Nepal.

3. Francis Buchanan Hamilton — *An Account of the Kingdom of Nepal, and of the Territories Annexed to this Dominion by the House of Gorkha* (1819). **Evidence target:** original 1819 pages, including the Introduction and later Kirat/Kichak passages; retain exact page references with the uploaded scan.

4. Brian Houghton Hodgson — *On the Kiranti Tribe*, Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal 27 (1859), pp. 447–456. **Evidence target:** original article pages.

5. Brian Houghton Hodgson — *Comparative Vocabulary of the several languages (dialects) of the celebrated people called Kirantis*, Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal 26 (1857), pp. 333–371. **Evidence target:** original article pages and/or Hodgson manuscript vocabulary.

6. Brian Houghton Hodgson — *Comparative Vocabulary of the Kiranti* manuscript, Royal Asiatic Society Archives, BHH/6/1/1. The archive describes comparative vocabulary for several Kiranti languages/dialects and places the material in the Arun basin/easternmost province of Nepal. **Evidence target:** manuscript images/archival reproduction.

7. Brian Houghton Hodgson — Limbu manuscripts and teaching materials in the Hodgson Papers, including religion/culture manuscripts, Limbu alphabet/language teaching material, descriptions of Kiranta territory, clans/family names, and related ethnographic notes. **Evidence target:** British Library/University of Cambridge Hodgson Papers item images and folios.

8. E. Vansittart — Kirat-related nineteenth-century material cited by Iman Singh Chemjong as one of the European authorities consulted for Kirat history and civilization. **Evidence target:** identify the exact original Vansittart work and page before entering any quotation.

9. Henry Ambrose Oldfield — *Sketches from Nipal* (1880). **Evidence target:** exact pages/plates relevant to Kirat/Kiranti peoples or eastern Nepal.

10. Daniel Wright — historical work on Nepal and related documentary material. **Evidence target:** exact pages where Kirata/Kiranti material is discussed.

11. Percival Landon — *Nepal* (1928). **Evidence target:** exact pages/chapters relevant to Kirat/Kiranti history and eastern Nepal.

12. Sylvain Lévi — *Le Népal* (1905–1908). **Evidence target:** exact volume/page for every Kirata/Kiranti claim used.

13. Toni Hagen — twentieth-century field/geographical documentation of Nepal. **Evidence target:** exact work/page for any Kirat/Kiranti-specific statement.

### B. Historical and archival documentary evidence

14. Documenta Nepalica — historical documents indexed under **Pallo Kirāta**. The catalogue records multiple historical documents, including entries dated 1780, 1819, 1834, and 1851. **Evidence target:** original digital document/folio for each selected item.

15. Documenta Nepalica / Regmi Research Collection — regulations concerning land surveys in **Majha-Kirāta and Pallo-Kirāta**, VS 1862. Identifier RRC_0006_0157; one folio; Nepali/Devanagari; catalogue and document references supplied by the project. **Evidence target:** original document image.

16. Documenta Nepalica / Regmi Research Collection — order concerning revenue collection in **Mājha and Pallo Kirāta**, VS 1906. Identifier RRC_0089_0038; two folios; Nepali/English, Devanagari; catalogue notes cite RRC vol. 89 no. 30, pp. 493–494. **Evidence target:** original document image.

17. Documents on Limbuvān — Documenta Nepalica collection. The project documents historical Limbu territory (Limbuvān/Pallo Kirāta), kipat, border conflicts, political power of Limbu headmen, and social institutions including marriage and adoption. **Evidence target:** each original document/folio used for a claim.

22. Philippe Sagant — research on Limbu political power, religion, and social organisation, including his published work on Limbu communities. **Evidence target:** exact article/book and page for each claim.

23. Martin Gaenszle — research on Kiranti oral tradition, Mundhum, ritual language, Limbu script, and religious/community formation. **Evidence target:** exact article/book/chapter and page for each claim.

24. Mélanie Vandenhelsken and Martin Gaenszle — research on Limbu religion and community formation. **Evidence target:** exact chapter/article and page.

25. Gérard Schlemmer — research on the historical reinvention/reconstruction of Kirant identity and related eastern Nepal materials. **Evidence target:** exact publication and page.

26. Charlotte Hardman — ethnographic research on Yakkha/Kiranti communities. **Evidence target:** exact publication and page.

27. Nicholas Allen — anthropological and comparative research relevant to Kiranti/Yakkha traditions and social organisation. **Evidence target:** exact publication and page.

28. Liliya A. Streltsova — “The ‘Kirat Religion’ in Nepal: Quest for a New Religious and Ethnic Identity” (2023), *Etnograficheskoe obozrenie* 4: 66–83. 29. Axel Michaels — *Nepal: A History from the Earliest Times to the Present* (2024). **Evidence target:** exact chapter/page for every Kirata/Kiranti historical statement used.

### D. Nepali / Kiranti scholarship

30. Iman Singh Chemjong — *Kirat Itihas* (first published 1948; later editions). **Evidence target:** exact edition and page.

The work states that Chemjong consulted European authors including Kirkpatrick, Hamilton, Hodgson, Father Giuseppe and E. Vansittart; Indian authors including Rahul Sankrityayan, S. K. Chatterjee and Bhagava Datta; and old Kirat manuscripts in Shrijunga/Limbu and Lepcha/Rong scripts. **Evidence target:** exact edition/page of Chemjong's methodological/source statement and every substantive claim.

32. Tanka Bahadur Rai — *Kirat Itihas*. **Evidence target:** exact edition/page.

33. Dor Bahadur Bista — *People of Nepal* (1967). Includes discussion relevant to Kiranti communities and uses earlier documentary sources. **Evidence target:** exact pages for every claim.

34. Mohan Kumar Tumbahang — doctoral research on Limbu Mundhum and related linguistic/cultural materials. **Evidence target:** exact thesis page for each claim.

35. Government / university catalogues and theses — including Tribhuvan University repositories and official library catalogues containing Kirat history, culture, religion, language, Mundhum, Rai, Limbu, Yakkha and Sunuwar studies. **Evidence target:** original thesis/book/article and exact page.

## Evidence classification

Use one of these evidence classes when entering a source into the Universal Research Workflow:

- Primary printed source
- Archival manuscript
- Government/administrative document
- Original map
- Original linguistic/lexicographic manuscript
- Academic monograph
- Peer-reviewed academic article
- Doctoral/MPhil thesis
- Ethnographic fieldwork
- Oral-history record
- Archaeological/material evidence
- Secondary scholarly synthesis

## Source-control rule

Do not merge the statements of different authors. If Hamilton reports Giuseppe, keep the statement attributed to Hamilton as Hamilton's report unless the original Giuseppe document has independently been located. If a modern scholar interprets an older source, record the modern interpretation separately from the older source.

## Evidence rule for the Editorial Console

For each Source 1, Source 2, Source 3...:

1. Author / Informant
2. Country / Institution / Archive
3. Work / Source
4. Publication year
5. Publisher / Repository
6. Edition
7. Chapter / Section
8. Exact page / folio
9. What the source says
10. Original quotation (only when verified)
11. Nepali translation
12. English explanation
13. Actual evidence attachment
14. Research assessment / note

Never label a claim as “Final Truth”, “True”, or “Verified Truth” merely because it appears in a source. Source presence and research verification are separate fields.
