## 5. Primary evidence
For each item, record source ID, object/document identity, provenance, exact locator, transcription/translation, dating basis, rights, and what it can and cannot establish.

## 6. Scholarly discussion
Compare relevant academic books, peer-reviewed articles, theses, excavation reports, and institutional catalogues. Record exact pages and stable identifiers.

## 7. Community knowledge and oral history
Record community and narrator attribution only with consent; language, location, date, interviewer, translator, recording ID, transmission context, and access restrictions. Distinguish cultural meaning from independently established chronology.

## 9. Claim-by-claim evidence
Give every substantive claim a stable Claim ID and enter it in `CLAIM_EVIDENCE_REGISTER_TEMPLATE.csv` or the maintained project register. One testable proposition per row.

## 10. Research conclusion
- Findings supported by the reviewed evidence:

## 11. References
Use Chicago Notes and Bibliography by default for historical writing. Include author/institution, title, edition/translator, publication details, exact page/folio/object ID, DOI or stable URL, access date where appropriate, language, and rights/licence.

## 12. Publication and revision checklist
- [ ] Every key claim has a Claim ID and exact source locator.
- [ ] Quotations/translations were checked against the source.
- [ ] At least one independent comparison is recorded, or absence is explained.
- [ ] Contradictory evidence and limitations are represented fairly.
- [ ] Community knowledge has attribution, consent, and context.
- [ ] Image/audio/video rights and provenance are recorded.
- [ ] The conclusion does not exceed the evidence.
- [ ] Reviewer, date, version, and revision history are recorded.
- [ ] No private credentials, sensitive personal data, or restricted knowledge are exposed.
