Therefore, this audit does **not** falsely label the collection “54/54 cross-checked.” The next authoritative total should be generated from one deduplicated master source register.

## 2. What “cross-checked” means here

A source-level cross-check means the source identity, author/institution, work/object, date, catalogue/reference, and available evidence target were checked against an authoritative catalogue/repository where possible.

A claim-level cross-check is stronger: the exact page/folio/document is read and compared with another source. A bibliography record alone does not establish what an author actually said.

A catalogue entry for a manuscript proves that the catalogue describes that object; it does not by itself prove every historical proposition that someone may later attribute to that manuscript.

## 4. Verified archival anchors from the current web cross-check

### Hamilton 1819
Library of Congress confirms the 1819 edition, 364-page volume, digital images/PDF, and describes Hamilton's Nepal work as based on his 1802–03 stay and conversations with hereditary chiefs and others. The LOC rights note says it is unaware of copyright or other restrictions in the World Digital Library collection and, absent such restrictions, the materials are free to use/reuse.

Evidence targets retained in the archive: pp. 2–3, 7–8, 133–134, 148–149.

### Kirkpatrick 1811
Nepal National Archives hosts a digitised PDF titled *An account of the kingdom of Nepal* by Kirkpatrick, described as derived from East India Company records and published under their sanction. Exact Kirata/Yalambar page-level quotations still require direct page verification.

### British Library EAP1023/21/5
Official catalogue: *किराँत धर्म / Kiranta Religious book*, 21 paper folios, 21 TIFF images, Kiranti language/Limbu script, catalogue date range 16th century, research access, legal status “Not Public Record(s).”

### British Library EAP1023/22/17
Official catalogue: *किराँत धर्म / Kiranta Book of Religion*, 25 paper folios, 27 TIFF images, Kiranti language/Limbu script, catalogue date range 16th century, research access, legal status “Not Public Record(s).”

### Hodgson Papers Mss Eur Hodgson/85
Official British Library catalogue: *Limbu manuscripts: texts of religion and culture*, dated 1846, one bound volume, pages numbered 1–278. The scope includes worship/prayer, cosmology, customs, rituals, marriage, agriculture and comparative vocabulary. Legal status is “Not Public Record(s).”

### Documenta Nepalica
- RRC_0006_0157: land-survey regulations in Majha-Kirāta and Pallo-Kirāta, VS 1862, 1 folio, RRC vol. 6 no. 157, pp. 156–162.
- RRC_0089_0038: revenue-collection order in Mājha and pallo Kirāta, 2 folios, RRC vol. 89 no. 30, pp. 493–494.
- RRC_0036_0252: appeal to Kirātīs and Lepchas across the Ṭistā, VS 1865, 2 folios, RRC vol. 36 no. 221, pp. 243–244.
- E_3420_0013: copy of a rukkā from King Rājendra, VS 1876 / 1819 CE, concerning officers at Pāñca Khapana in Pallo Kirāta/Limbuvān; the edition/translation/commentary is CC BY-SA 4.0, while facsimile copyright remains with the National Archives and prior written permission is required for digital facsimiles.
- The Pallo Kirāta ontology lists reliable records E_3345_0008 (1851), E_3420_0007 (VS 1885), E_3420_0008 (1780/VS 1837), E_3420_0013 (1819/VS 1876), and E_3420_0016 (1834/VS 1891).

### TU / Mundhum
Tribhuvan University Central Library hosts Ramesh Kumar Limbu's 2010 *Performance in Limbu Mundhum: A Study of Cultural Representation* and provides the thesis PDF. The abstract describes Mundhum as religious narrative material transmitted orally and studies its performance and socio-cultural/religio-historical context. This remains the author's scholarly framing and must not be generalized beyond the study without additional sources.

### Streltsova 2023
The journal record verifies Liliya Streltsova's article, issue 4 (2023), pp. 66–83, DOI 10.31857/S0869541523040048. The abstract discusses Limbu, Rai, Yakha and Sunuwar as part of the wider Kiranti ethnic context and describes Mundhum as an oral-text complex in the article's scholarly framework.

## 5. Evidence attachment rule

For every substantive claim retained in the public research archive:

1. Keep author/informant attribution.
2. Record institution/archive/country.
3. Record exact work/document/interview.
4. Record publication/document date.
5. Record edition/publisher where applicable.
6. Record exact page/folio/section.
7. Record what the source actually says.
8. Attach the original evidence file where legally reusable.
9. If the source is restricted/copyrighted, retain citation + exact target + rights status and do not redistribute the restricted facsimile without permission.
10. Keep researcher analysis and final conclusion separate from source wording.

## 6. Current status of the “all sources” request

The archive now has a stronger source-control basis, but it is **not correct to claim that every one of the 52 bibliography entries has had every historical statement extracted, compared, and independently verified**. Many entries are bibliographically identified and still require claim-level page/folio work.

The same applies to the 45 structured registry records: “primary_evidence_identified” means the original evidence object/source has been identified; it does not mean that a lawful copy is already stored or that every possible claim from the source is verified.

This distinction is permanent under the Master Rules.
