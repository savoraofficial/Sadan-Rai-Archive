# MASTER EVIDENCE — WHAT EACH CURRENT SOURCE SAFELY ESTABLISHES
## Sadan Rai — History, Civilization & Culture Archive
### Audit date: 2026-10-03

## Purpose
This is a source-bounded evidence map for the 45-record structured source register. It does **not** convert bibliography/catalogue records into proof of every historical proposition. Each entry distinguishes what is currently safe to state from what still requires exact page/folio/record reading.

## Master rule
- A catalogue record proves the existence/description of the catalogued object, not every historical claim attributed to it.
- A bibliographic identification proves that the work/source exists, not that every statement in the work has been checked.
- A primary-evidence identification means the underlying evidence object has been identified; it does not mean a lawful copy is stored in this archive.
- Restricted/copyrighted material is not redistributed merely because it is digitised.
- Oral testimony is recorded as testimony and then independently cross-checked; it is not automatically converted into established historical fact.

## Current evidence conclusion
The current archive supports a **multi-layer evidence model** rather than a single definitive narrative:
1. Early-19th-century documentary evidence independently records Kirat/Kichak communities in eastern Nepal in Hamilton's account.
2. British Library catalogues preserve multiple Kiranta religious/story/prayer manuscript objects with catalogue dates reaching the 15th–17th centuries; these are archival objects and require folio-level reading before content claims are made.
3. Documenta Nepalica preserves multiple later historical records explicitly associated with Pallo Kirāta/Limbuvān and Kirātīs.
4. Modern scholarship documents oral ritual/Mundhum and Kiranti/Limbu cultural traditions in defined research contexts.
5. Yellung/Ya’lamba/Yalambar remains a chronicle/traditional-historical subject in the present evidence base; exact ancient identity/date claims must not be upgraded to contemporary historical proof without stronger evidence.
