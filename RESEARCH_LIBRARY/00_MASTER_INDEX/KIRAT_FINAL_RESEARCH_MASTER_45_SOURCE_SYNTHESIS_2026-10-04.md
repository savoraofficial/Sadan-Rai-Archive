# Kirat Final Research — 45-Source Evidence-Bounded Synthesis

**Cut-off:** 2026-10-04  \n**Scope:** all 45 current structured source/research records in the archive.

## Critical rule
This is a **100% inventory/source-status audit of all 45 records**, not a claim that every historical proposition is proven. A source can be fully identified while a particular historical claim remains page/folio-pending. The final research uses only statements whose evidence boundary is explicit.

## 1. किराँत कालको परिचय / Introduction to Kirat Era
The current evidence supports a careful distinction between (a) documentary evidence for Kirat/Kiranti communities in historical Nepal and (b) later chronicle traditions about very early Kirata rulers. Hamilton's 1819 account provides direct early-nineteenth-century documentary evidence for a Kirat/Kichak population east of Nepal Proper and identifies information obtained from named local informants. The archive's stored Hamilton extract targets pp. 2–3, 7–8, 133–134 and 148–149. Kirkpatrick's 1811 work is independently identified but its exact Kirat pages still require extraction.

**Final status:** documented/source-bounded for the early-19th-century documentary layer; ancient dynastic claims remain chronicle/traditional unless independently supported.

**Key source IDs:** #2, #3, #10, #12, #29, #30, #31, #32, #35, #36, #37, #45.

## 2. ऐतिहासिक कालक्रम / Historical Timeline
The archive contains dated documentary layers rather than one uninterrupted proven chronology: British Library catalogue-dated Kiranta manuscript objects (including 15th–16th-century catalogue dates), Pallo Kirāta records spanning selected 18th–19th-century records, RRC land-survey and revenue documents, and 19th-century ethnographic/linguistic scholarship. Catalogue dates establish the archival object's recorded date range; they do not by themselves prove the date of every statement inside a manuscript.

**Final status:** chronology is evidence-layered and source-specific; no single continuous ancient timeline is claimed as proven.

**Key source IDs:** #6, #7, #14–17, #38–40, #42–44.

## 3. ऐतिहासिक ग्रन्थ तथा विद्वत् दृष्टिकोण / Authors & Researchers
The archive preserves multiple scholarly perspectives: Hodgson's nineteenth-century linguistic/ethnographic materials; Hamilton and Kirkpatrick's early accounts; Caplan's study of Hindu–Limbu land/social relations; Hansson/Winter's linguistic survey; Gaenszle's Mewahang Rai ritual-text research; van Driem's Limbu grammar; Sagant's study of Limbu chiefs; Streltsova's 2023 analysis of changing Kirat religious identity; Michaels' 2024 general history of Nepal; and Nepali Kirat historiography including Chemjong and Tanka Bahadur Rai. These are not interchangeable levels of evidence. Scholarly interpretation is kept distinct from primary-document evidence.

**Final status:** multiple perspectives are documented; exact page-level claim extraction remains pending for several works.

**Key source IDs:** #4, #5, #18–33, #37, #41.

## 4. प्रमाण अभिलेख / Evidence & Documentary Archive
The strongest current evidence layer consists of identifiable primary/documentary objects: Hamilton's printed pages; Hodgson's archival vocabulary manuscript; British Library EAP Kiranta manuscripts; Hodgson/85 Limbu manuscripts; Documenta Nepalica Pallo/Majha Kirāta records; and the RRC appeal/land/revenue documents. Some are restricted or catalogue-only. A catalogue record is not a substitute for reading the actual folio.

**Final status:** primary objects are identified across the archive; only a subset has page-level text already extracted. Restricted material must not be redistributed without permission.

**Highest current page-level evidence:** Hamilton #3/#35.
**Strong catalogue/object evidence:** #6, #7, #14–17, #38–44.

## 5. किराँत सभ्यता / Kirat Civilization
The evidence supports studying Kirat/Kiranti civilization through documented communities, social institutions, language, ritual traditions, land relations, political administration, and material/documentary heritage. Caplan provides a modern scholarly analysis of Limbu land/social relations; Hansson/Winter and van Driem document linguistic dimensions; Gaenszle and Ramesh Kumar Limbu document oral/ritual performance in specific communities; Documenta Nepalica provides historical administrative/legal records; and British Library/Hodgson collections document manuscript traditions. These sources should not be generalized into one uniform culture for every Kiranti community.

**Final status:** supported as a multi-community research field; community-specific attribution required.

## 6. मौखिक इतिहास / Oral History
Modern scholarship documents Mundhum and oral ritual traditions in specific Kiranti communities. Gaenszle's Mewahang Rai study and Ramesh Kumar Limbu's Limbu Mundhum thesis are especially relevant. The archive treats oral tradition as an independent evidence category: an oral account is preserved as testimony/tradition unless independent documentary, archaeological, linguistic or other evidence supports a stronger historical conclusion.

**Final status:** supported as a living oral-tradition/research domain; not automatically proof of ancient historical events.

## 7. विवादित तथा थप अनुसन्धान आवश्यक विषय / Disputed & Further Research
The current controlled register keeps the following unresolved: Yellung/Ya'lamba/Yalambar as a first Kirata ruler is preserved in chronicle/traditional historiography but is not established here as contemporary ancient history; Yalambar as a specific Mahabharata character is not independently established; the alleged ancient title “Hang” for Yalambar remains research-required; exact contents of several British Library manuscripts require folio-level reading; several older scholarly works require exact page extraction; and field/oral verification remains pending.

**Final status:** disputed/unresolved items remain explicitly labelled and are not promoted to proven facts.
